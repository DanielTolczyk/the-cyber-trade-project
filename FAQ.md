# Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### 1. Does this replace a four-year computer science degree?
No. Academic degrees remain valuable for theoretical computer science and research. However, operational cybersecurity is an applied engineering trade. The trade pipeline provides verified hands-on competency and structured career progression without requiring arbitrary degree gatekeeping or unmanageable student debt.

### 2. How are existing experienced professionals grandfathered in?
Active professionals transition into the trade through a **24-Month Legacy Transition Window**:
* **Journeyman Standing:** 5+ years of verified operational defense experience (10,000+ hours) or holding a Board-recognized multi-domain credential (such as the CISSP) grants licensure upon portfolio review and ethics exam completion. Grandfathering runtime requirements are higher than standard JATC tracks to compensate for the absence of rotational logbooks.
* **Master Practitioner Standing:** 10+ years of verified operational experience (20,000+ hours) evaluated through an oral board defense, incident history audit, and safety sign-off assessment.
* **Prior Learning Assessment (PLA) Credits:** Documented systems administration or network engineering experience qualifies for up to 2,000 hours (Tier 2 entry). Accredited military cyber occupational specialties (MOS/AFSC) qualify for up to 4,000 hours (Tier 3 entry). A mandatory 50% (4,000-hour) live production residency floor applies to all candidates.

### 3. Does this invalidate my existing certifications?
No. The Board operates a **Credential Evaluation Clearinghouse** that audits established certifications against open standards (such as NIST NICE). Validated practical credentials grant credit toward Related Technical Instruction (RTI) classroom hours and rotational milestones, preserving existing investments while filtering out low-rigor multiple-choice exams.

### 4. Will I be personally sued if my company gets breached?
No. Financial losses, ransomware claims, and business interruptions are covered by the **Enterprise Cyber Liability Policy**. Frontline engineers operate under full statutory safe harbor for good-faith work. Personal Errors & Omissions (E&O) applies strictly to intentional sabotage, deliberate fraud, or statutory gross negligence. Furthermore, filing a formal refusal when overruled on safety baselines transfers liability directly to executive leadership.

### 5. How does this prevent small businesses (SMBs) from being priced out?
Small and medium-sized businesses utilize **Fractional Masters of Record (vMoR)** through accredited **Licensed Cybersecurity Trade Contractors** (MSSPs or boutique consultancies). Similar to retaining an independent Master Electrician to inspect blueprints and sign off on permits, SMBs obtain certified oversight and compliance stamps without full-time executive payroll overhead.

### 6. Will this slow down agile development and modern CI/CD pipelines?
No. Headcount ratios apply strictly to human-in-the-loop operational seats (such as live SOC triage or manual penetration testing). For software development, Masters of Record certify the **automated CI/CD pipelines, policy-as-code guardrails, and runtime admission controllers**. Once certified, development teams deploy continuously and autonomously without ticket-based gates.

### 7. How does liability work with cloud providers (AWS, Azure, GCP)?
Liability strictly follows the Cloud Shared Responsibility Model. Licensed practitioners are accountable only for customer-controlled assets (IAM policies, tenant configurations, encryption baselines, egress controls). Practitioners are fully shielded from upstream hypervisor vulnerabilities, hardware faults, or provider outages.

### 8. How are remote, interstate, and offshore teams handled?
* **National Interstate Compact (NCTLC):** Modeled after the Interstate Medical Licensure Compact, a digital interstate compact ensures licenses and logged hours remain portable across state lines for remote workers.
* **Global & Contractor Teams:** Offshore teams, third-party contractors, and MSPs operate under the structured supervision and statutory sign-off authority of a licensed Journeyman or Master of Record.

### 9. What about self-taught engineers and non-traditional talent?
The framework guarantees merit-based advancement through **Practical Challenge Examinations** and **Prior Learning Assessments (PLA)**. Anyone with demonstrated hands-on proficiency can test directly into advanced apprentice tiers or challenge the Journeyman exam. Bug bounty disclosures, CVEs, military service, and lab-based credentials translate into standardized hour credits (up to the 50% residency cap). For complete rules, see the [Apprenticeship Standards](framework/apprenticeship-standards.md).

### 10. Will this restrict open-source development or vulnerability research?
No. Licensure regulates administrative root access, production deployments, and statutory compliance sign-offs over **commercial production systems and critical infrastructure**. It does not apply to open-source development, independent vulnerability research, academic study, or home lab experimentation.

### 11. Is the Craft Guild just a traditional union?
The Guild functions as a professional craft association and multi-employer training trust modeled after skilled trade associations (such as the IBEW/NECA JATC) and professional boards. It administers zero-tuition JATC training trusts, provides legal defense for practitioners exercising technical refusal, manages portable benefits, and guarantees workforce competency. Licensure itself is administered independently by the National Board, ensuring open-shop practitioners practice freely.

### 12. Can I work as a 1099 contractor or launch my own consulting business?
Yes. Licensed Journeymen and Masters hold full professional mobility to work as 1099 contractors, staff-augmentation specialists, or establish an independent consulting firm as a **Licensed Cybersecurity Trade Contractor**. Contracting entities register a designated Master of Record, carry commercial surety and E&O coverage, and are authorized to sponsor JATC apprentices and provide fractional compliance sign-offs. Registered Apprentices must remain W-2 employees (via an employer or the JATC Trust) to ensure statutory training oversight.

### 13. What is the "Right of Technical Refusal"?
The Right of Technical Refusal ensures licensed practitioners cannot be forced to deploy unsafe systems, bypass mandatory security controls, or sign off on unverified checklists under administrative pressure. Frontline engineers (Apprentices and Journeymen) raise internal **Operational Exception Flags (Form FORM-003)**, while designated **Masters of Record (MoR)** hold statutory authority to file a formal **Notice of Safety Non-Concurrence (Form FORM-001)**.

### 14. Does a Notice of Safety Non-Concurrence stop an enterprise from operating?
No. In commercial enterprises, the framework does not seize executive decision-making authority. It functions as an **evidentiary liability transfer mechanism**. If executive leadership chooses to accept the technical risk, they proceed by executing a formal **Executive Override & Risk Acceptance Form (Form FORM-002)**.

### 15. How does the liability transfer protect frontline engineers?
When an Executive Override is executed, statutory, regulatory, and financial liability for incidents arising from the overridden defect transfers entirely to the overriding corporate officers. The Master of Record, supervising Journeymen, and frontline Apprentices are legally shielded by the Trade Ethical Shield and backed by the Guild Legal Defense Trust against retaliatory termination or administrative penalties.

### 16. How is the evidentiary trail protected if an employer attempts to suppress it?
When a Notice of Safety Non-Concurrence is filed, its cryptographic hash is dual-logged to the enterprise internal audit log and the National Board clearinghouse. This creates an immutable, timestamped record of refusal and technical findings outside corporate control that cannot be altered or suppressed internally following a breach.

### 17. How can cybersecurity be standardized when vendor tooling varies so widely across enterprises?
This dynamic exists across all skilled trades. An electrician works across Square D, Siemens, and Eaton panels; an HVAC technician services Trane, Carrier, and Lennox units; an aviation mechanic repairs Boeing and Airbus systems. The proprietary interface changes, but the underlying engineering principles (Ohm's law, circuit design, thermodynamics, and safety codes) remain universal.

In cybersecurity, whether configuring Checkpoint, Palo Alto Networks, Cisco, AWS Security Groups, or Linux nftables, the foundational engineering principles are identical: TCP/IP state mechanics, packet inspection, CIDR routing, cryptographic key exchanges, authentication handshakes, and zero-trust isolation. The framework baselines vendor-neutral competencies mapped to open standards (NIST NICE and NIST CSF), while vendor-specific products sit on top as modular tooling.

### 18. Doesn't the rapid pace of technological change make a multi-year trade framework obsolete?
No. While threat tactics and cloud APIs evolve continuously, the underlying defensive engineering primitives (least privilege, cryptographic assurance, memory safety, logging telemetry, and identity governance) have remained consistent for decades. The framework pairs a 2-year generalist core with modular Specialty Endorsement tracks (such as AI/ML Assurance, Cloud Security, and ICS/SCADA) and an 18-Month JATC Curriculum Notice Rule, enabling rapid integration of new technical domains without disrupting foundational stability.

### 19. Does a standardized trade model create a centralized monopoly that restricts free-market competition?
No. High-consequence professions (medicine, civil engineering, aviation, and electrical trades) operate in competitive free markets with mandatory public safety baselines. The independent National Board sets objective standards but does not sell training; community colleges, universities, non-profit JATC trusts, private providers, and enterprise academies compete freely to deliver instruction. Furthermore, the open-shop model ensures non-guild practitioners practice freely, while direct clearinghouse hiring cuts out 20% to 30% third-party staffing markups.

### 20. What happens to an apprentice if their sponsoring employer downsizes, is acquired, or closes?
Under the skilled trade framework, an apprentice's career pipeline outlives any individual employer. The apprentice permanently owns their digital logbook and accumulated runtime hours in the National Clearinghouse. If an employer experiences a contraction or closure, the multi-employer JATC training trust immediately reallocates and dispatches the apprentice to another participating sponsor facing a labor deficit, preserving their progress toward the 8,000-hour Journeyman milestone.
