# The Cyber Trade Project

> Transforming cybersecurity workforce development from an academic-first model into a verified, guild-governed trade.


---

## The Model at a Glance

* **The Lever:** Cyber liability insurance underwriters mandate verified operational standards for lower deductibles and premium credits.
* **The Adoption:** Enterprises sponsor apprenticeship seats because the insurance premium offset makes training cost-neutral or net-positive.
* **The Authority:** The Guild owns the credential, verified runtime hours, and competency audits. Academic institutions serve as contracted training providers to the guild standard, not the gatekeepers.

## The Core Pillars

### Pillar 1: Verified Seat Time Over Academic Theory
Practical operational proficiency requires logged, audited hours in live environments—not standardized multiple-choice exams.

### Pillar 2: The Apprentice-to-Master Progression
Clear, non-predatory career tiers (Apprentice → Journeyman → Master) anchored by transparent wage minimums and supervisor sign-offs.

### Pillar 3: Zero-Debt Vocational Pipelines
Apprentices earn while they learn. Sponsoring organizations fund technical training as an operational overhead offset by insurance credits.

### Pillar 4: Portable, Standardized Credentialing
Qualifications belong to the tradesperson, not a proprietary vendor or specific corporate silo.

### Pillar 5: Safety & Ethical Codes of Practice
Enforceable codes of conduct and liability standards modeled after structural engineering and master electrician boards.

### Pillar 6: Collective Governance & Guild Architecture
A unified body to govern training standards, negotiate baseline wages, and maintain the public trust.

### Pillar 7: The Economic Insurance Catalyst
Underwriters establish the cyber trade credential as a risk-reduction benchmark, creating hard economic incentives for enterprise adoption.
