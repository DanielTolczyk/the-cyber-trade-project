# National Governance Board & Regulatory Administration

The National Cybersecurity Trade Board is an independent, self-regulating regulatory entity responsible for public safety, credential standards, and professional licensing. It operates under statutory authority, strictly independent from both individual corporate employers and the Cybersecurity Craft Guild.

> **Foundational Principle:** The Board licenses the practitioner and protects the public interest; the Craft Guild trains and represents the workforce.

---

## 1. Multilateral Board Composition
To prevent regulatory capture, anti-competitive closed shops, or corporate dilution of standards, the Board maintains a strictly balanced, multi-stakeholder composition:

* **25% Craft Guild & Labor Representatives:** Peer-elected Journeymen and Master Practitioners representing trade members and apprenticeship training trusts (JATCs).
* **25% Enterprise & Commercial Employers:** CISO council delegates, commercial engineering leaders, and open-shop enterprise representatives.
* **20% Cyber-Insurance Underwriters & Actuaries:** Risk modeling experts representing corporate underwriting consortiums and reinsurers.
* **15% Academic & Vocational Leaders:** Curriculum deans from accredited community colleges, technical universities, and registered apprenticeship sponsors.
* **15% Public Interest, Legal & Safety Observers:** Consumer privacy advocates, legal ethicists, and critical infrastructure safety liaisons.

---

## 2. Separation of Powers & Non-Discrimination
* **Universal Licensure Standard:** The Board evaluates candidates purely on documented logbook hours, competency assessments, and ethics reviews. Licensure is open to all qualified individuals regardless of union or Guild affiliation.
* **Independent Practical Examinations:** The Board administers and audits vendor-neutral practical milestone challenge exams, ensuring curricula remain objective and tied to open standards (e.g., NIST NICE) rather than commercial vendor marketing.
* **Program Accreditation:** The Board audits and accredits training curricula delivered by both Guild-run JATC facilities, community colleges, and independent corporate apprenticeship programs.

---

## 3. Core Administrative Mandates

### 1. Licensure Registry & Interstate Reciprocity Compact
* Operating the national, tamper-evident digital ledger of licensed apprentices, Journeymen, and Master Practitioners.
* Administering the Interstate Reciprocity Compact to ensure trade licenses and operational hours remain fully portable across employers and state boundaries for remote practitioners.

### 2. Credential Evaluation Clearinghouse
* Conducting rigorous, periodic audits of commercial and academic security credentials against open trade standards.
* Determining official apprenticeship tier placements, Related Technical Instruction (RTI) classroom credits, and domain equivalencies while filtering out unverified multiple-choice brain dumps.

### 3. Malpractice Inquiries & License Sanctions
* Conducting formal evidentiary hearings regarding allegations of gross negligence, willful bypass of security controls, audit falsification, or malicious insider actions.
* Upholding strict due process, requiring clear and convincing evidence to prevent frivolous employer retaliation.
* Issuing binding professional sanctions: formal reprimand, mandatory retraining, license suspension, or permanent license revocation.

### 4. Refusal Dispute Adjudication
* Serving as the independent appellate body when an employer disputes a licensed practitioner's formal **Notice of Safety Non-Concurrence**.
* Evaluating whether an engineer's technical refusal was justified under recognized safety, architectural, and regulatory baselines, and issuing legally binding determinations of safe harbor.
