# Standards, Regulatory Baselines & Compliance Architecture

The Cybersecurity Trade Project does not invent proprietary security controls or dictate business operations. Instead, it adopts the proven "Building Code" model of the skilled trades: aligning workforce training with open industry standards and transforming checkbox compliance into verifiable engineering attestations.

---

## 1. The Building Code Hierarchy

1. **Regulatory Statutes (The Law):** Federal and sector laws such as SEC Disclosures, HIPAA, FDA 524B, and NERC-CIP. Enforced by government regulators.
2. **Technical Baselines (The Building Codes):** Open technical control baselines such as NIST CSF / SP 800-53, CIS Critical Security Controls, ISO/IEC 27001, and IEC 62443. Selected by the enterprise and audited by licensed trade labor.
3. **Workforce & Training Taxonomy (The Trade):** The NIST NICE Framework (SP 800-181). Governs apprentice logbooks, core rotations, and Board examination milestones.

---

## 2. Workforce Alignment: NIST NICE Taxonomy
All apprentice work-role descriptions, rotational learning objectives, and logbook hours are mapped to the **National Initiative for Cybersecurity Education (NICE) Framework (NIST SP 800-181)**:
* **Rotational Domains:** Directly mirror NICE Category modules (Securely Provision, Operate & Maintain, Protect & Defend, Analyze, Oversee & Govern).
* **Federal Recognition:** Ensures participating employers qualify for federal/state workforce development grants and registered apprenticeship tax incentives.

---

## 3. Technical Baselines: Enterprise Discretion
Enterprises maintain full autonomy to select the technical frameworks appropriate for their business model and risk tolerance:
* **Commercial / General Tech:** CIS Critical Security Controls, NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF), ISO/IEC 27001.
* **Industrial & Critical Infrastructure:** IEC 62443, NERC-CIP.
* **Application Security:** OWASP Software Assurance Maturity Model (SAMM), ASVS.

The trade guarantees that licensed Journeymen and Masters are trained to build, harden, and inspect systems according to these open baselines.

---

## 4. Professional Attestation & Eliminating "Paper Compliance"
Regulatory filings and third-party risk attestations carry legal consequence. Under the trade framework:
* **Technical Sign-Off:** Routine audit findings, control validations, and compliance readiness reports require review and verification by an on-site or fractional licensed Journeyman or Master of Record.
* **Liability Alignment:** Signing off on known falsified controls constitutes professional malpractice, subjecting the individual to Board investigation and license suspension.
