# Leave, Benefit Standards & Multi-Employer FMLA Equivalency

This standard defines the rules governing worker leave, wage replacement, and statutory protections across the cybersecurity trade. It establishes the jurisdictional boundary between employer-retained benefits and multi-employer labor trusts, creates trade-wide FMLA equivalency for participating organizations, and defines protections for registered apprentices and 1099 independent contractors.

---

## 1. Statutory Context & Regulatory Scope

Federal leave statutes create coverage gaps that disfavor cybersecurity workers and small-to-medium businesses:

* **The FMLA Structural Gap:** The federal Family and Medical Leave Act (29 U.S.C. § 2601 et seq.) applies exclusively to organizations employing 50 or more workers within a 75-mile radius, and conditions eligibility upon 12 months and 1,250 hours of continuous tenure at a single enterprise. This leaves thousands of practitioners at startups, boutique consultancies, and regional MSSPs without statutory protection. Furthermore, federal FMLA provides strictly unpaid leave.
* **The High-Mobility Dilemma:** Cybersecurity practitioners frequently transition across enterprise environments, defense contracts, and managed services. Single-employer tenure requirements force practitioners to restart eligibility clocks repeatedly, penalizing family planning and mid-career medical recovery.
* **The Small Employer Financial Burden:** Direct-employer mandates that require small firms (under 50 personnel) to self-fund 12 to 16 weeks of full salary create catastrophic cash-flow exposure and encourage subconscious demographic bias during hiring.

Under Section 302(c) of the Labor Management Relations Act (LMRA, 29 U.S.C. § 186(c)) and the Employee Retirement Income Security Act (ERISA, 29 U.S.C. § 1002 et seq.), the Cybersecurity Craft Guild and Participating Employer Council (PEC) establish multi-employer benefit trusts. These trusts mutualize risk, eliminate corporate balance-sheet exposure, and provide continuous, portable leave coverage across the trade.

## 2. The Two-Tier Jurisdictional Boundary

To avoid administrative friction and preserve employer operational flexibility, benefits are divided into two clear operational divisions:

```
+─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
|               TWO-DIVISION JURISDICTIONAL BENEFIT ARCHITECTURE              |
+─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
  DIVISION A: EMPLOYER-RETAINED OBLIGATIONS (Direct Payroll & Shift Operations)
  • Scope: Absences under 5 consecutive business days.
  • Benefits: Routine PTO, floating holidays, incidental sick days (10-15 days/yr),
              and short-term post-incident DFIR rest cycles.
  • Administration: Employer HR and production scheduling; zero trust red tape.
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  DIVISION B: MULTI-EMPLOYER TRUST OBLIGATIONS (Taft-Hartley ERISA Fiduciary Pool)
  • Scope: Absences of 5 or more consecutive business days.
  • Benefits: Paid Parental Leave (12 wks), Short-Term Disability (26 wks),
              Health & Welfare continuity (Hour-Bank), Long-Term Disability,
              and extended post-incident trauma recovery.
  • Administration: Bonded Third-Party Administrator (TPA); independent of employer.
+─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
```

### A. Division A: Employer-Retained Obligations
1. **Incidental Sick Days & PTO:** Participating employers provide a baseline floor of employer-funded paid time off (minimum 10 paid days annually for full-time W-2 staff) to cover short-term personal illness, family care, and rest.
2. **Operational Shift Scheduling:** Shift coverage, pager rotation swaps, and planned vacation approvals remain under the managerial discretion of the employer.
3. **Mandatory Post-Incident Rest Cycles:** In accordance with Pillar VI (Section 4), mandatory operational recovery rest cycles following high-stress incident containment engagements (DFIR activations under 5 consecutive days) are fully compensable shift duty time administered directly by the employer.
4. **Absence Threshold:** Any absence extending beyond 4 consecutive business days transitions to Division B (Multi-Employer Trust Domain) under medical or qualifying certification.

### B. Division B: Multi-Employer Trust Obligations
1. **Health Insurance Continuity:** While a worker is on approved parental, medical, or family leave, the Taft-Hartley Health & Welfare Trust maintains comprehensive family medical, dental, and vision coverage through the worker's accumulated **Hour-Bank Reserve** (up to 6 months).
2. **Paid Parental Leave:** Disbursed through the Paid Family & Medical Leave (PFML) Sub-Trust, providing wage replacement for birth, adoption, or foster placement.
3. **Short-Term Disability (STD) & Incident Trauma Recovery:** Disbursed through the PFML Sub-Trust for non-occupational illness, surgery, mental health treatment, or extended DFIR trauma recovery extending beyond 7 calendar days.
4. **ERISA Insulation & Medical Privacy:** Claims are adjudicated by an independent Third-Party Administrator (TPA). Direct supervisors receive verification of approved leave duration but have no access to confidential medical records or clinical diagnoses.

## 3. Trust Economics & Wage Replacement Floors

The Taft-Hartley PFML & Disability Sub-Trust provides standardized wage replacement financed across the signatory employer base:

```
+─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
|              PFML SUB-TRUST REMITTANCE & DISBURSEMENT FLOW                  |
+─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
  PARTICIPATING EMPLOYERS (PEC)
  • Remit fixed hourly contribution of $0.75 per billable labor hour worked.
         │
         ▼
  TAFT-HARTLEY PFML & DISABILITY SUB-TRUST (ERISA Section 403 Fiduciary Pool)
         │
         ├─► Paid Parental Leave: 12 weeks @ 70% Regional Prevailing Baseline (RJPB)
         ├─► Short-Term Disability: Up to 26 weeks @ 70% RJPB (after 7-day wait)
         └─► State PFL Integration: Secondary wrap-around topping up statutory caps
+─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
```

### A. Employer Remittance Mandate
Every participating employer remits a mandatory **$0.75 per billable labor hour worked** by licensed Journeymen and Masters into the Taft-Hartley PFML Sub-Trust. These funds are segregated into an ERISA-shielded trust account, protected from corporate bankruptcy or commercial claims.

### B. The 1,000-Hour Multi-Employer Qualification Standard
To qualify for trust-administered paid leave:
* **Qualification Rule:** A practitioner must log at least **1,000 verified operational hours** within the preceding 12 calendar months.
* **Cross-Employer Accumulation:** Hours worked across any combination of participating employers, MSSP deployments, or accredited contractor gigs pool toward the 1,000-hour threshold.
* **Elimination of the Tenure Trap:** Practitioners moving between employers retain active eligibility without waiting periods.

### C. Benefit Replacement Rates & Ceilings
* **Paid Parental Leave:** 12 consecutive weeks of wage replacement calculated at **70% of the practitioner's active baseline wage step, capped at 70% of the Regional Journeyman Prevailing Baseline (RJPB)** for the worker's home chapter. This formula guarantees proportional income replacement for apprentices without creating wage step inversions (e.g., a Tier 1 apprentice receives 70% of their 50% RJPB rate, or 35% RJPB; a Journeyman receives 70% of RJPB).
* **Short-Term Disability & DFIR Trauma Recovery:** Up to 26 weeks of wage replacement calculated at **70% of the practitioner's active baseline wage step (capped at 70% RJPB)**, beginning on the 8th consecutive day of certified incapacity or physician-attested trauma recovery.
* **Statutory State Integration (Top-Up Wrap-Around):** In states maintaining statutory Paid Family and Medical Leave programs (e.g., California, Washington, New York, Massachusetts, New Jersey, Colorado, Oregon), the state fund serves as primary payer. The Guild Trust serves as a secondary wrap-around payer, disbursing the differential between the state statutory maximum and the 70% baseline trade standard.

## 4. Multi-Employer FMLA Equivalency & Re-entry Rights

To provide security across organizations of all sizes, all Participating Employer Council (PEC) members agree to enforce the **Multi-Employer FMLA Equivalency Standard**:

1. **Job Restoration Mandate:** Employers must restore an employee returning from qualified trust leave (up to 12 weeks for parental leave; up to 26 weeks for medical leave) to their prior position or to an equivalent position with identical pay, title, and supervisory standing.
2. **Downsizing Safety Valve (Priority Clearinghouse Dispatch):** If an employer executes a verified organizational reduction-in-force or loses a major client defense contract while an employee is on leave:
   * The employer must demonstrate that the position was eliminated for non-pretextual economic reasons unrelated to the leave.
   * The displaced worker is automatically designated for **Priority Dispatch Status** within the National Clearinghouse Registry.
   * The worker continues to receive trust wage replacement and Hour-Bank health coverage during dispatch placement.
3. **Prohibition of Retaliation:** Denying promotions, assigning degraded shift schedules, or lowering performance evaluations due to the exercise of qualified leave violates Ethical Canon 5, subjecting the employer to PEC compliance review and loss of preferred insurance discount tiers.

## 5. Protections for 1099 Contractors & Solo Practitioners

Independent contractors and solo trade contractors cannot access traditional employer leave policies. The framework provides structural parity through four specific mechanisms:

1. **Mandatory Loaded Wage Index:** Signatory clients contracting 1099 Journeymen or Masters must contract at or above the Loaded Trade Multiplier (1.35x to 1.45x the prevailing wage scale). This rate incorporates self-employment tax parity and a built-in 10% to 12% benefit factor to fund independent sick days and casual time off.
2. **Vacation and Sick Trust Sub-Accounts:** Contracts may specify a **$3.50 per hour Vacation and Sick Stamp** remitted by the client into the contractor's individual Guild sub-account. The contractor may draw down accumulated reserves during personal leave or receive automated quarterly disbursements.
3. **Trust PFML Parity:** Independent contractors who remit the $0.75 per hour PFML trust contribution and log 1,000 verified hours across signatory contracts within 12 months access the identical 12-week parental and 26-week disability wage replacement benefits as W-2 employees.
4. **Clearinghouse Employer of Record (EOR) Option:** Independent practitioners executing contract work may deliver services through the Guild Clearinghouse Hiring Hall, which functions as the statutory W-2 employer of record, administering taxes and benefits directly.

## 6. Apprentice Protections: The NCTB "Pause-and-Resume" Mandate

Apprentices who experience family changes or serious illness must not suffer career setbacks or forfeiture of accrued training:

1. **Cryptographic Hour Preservation:** All verified operational hours, ticket-hash attestations, and domain competencies recorded in the Universal Digital Logbook remain permanently valid. Accrued hours do not expire during leave.
2. **Rotational Clock Freeze:** The apprentice rotational clock freezes upon formal notice of leave and resumes immediately upon re-entry.
3. **Supervisory Ratio Maintenance:** Employers may not count an apprentice on leave against active on-shift supervisory ratios, ensuring active shifts maintain the mandatory 2:1 ratio.
4. **Zero-Tuition Hold:** JATC classroom instruction schedules pause during medical or parental leave, guaranteeing re-enrollment in the next semester cohort without reapplication fees or academic penalties.

4. **ERISA Insulation & Medical Privacy:** Claims are adjudicated by an independent Third-Party Administrator (TPA). Direct supervisors receive verification of approved leave duration but have no access to confidential medical records or clinical diagnoses.
