# Pillar III: Progressive Rotations & Enforced Ratios

A primary failure mode of modern security organizations is trapping junior analysts in narrow operational silos (e.g., Tier 1 SOC alert triage for 3+ years). This causes rapid burnout and starves the market of well-rounded senior talent.

---

## 1. Mandatory Cross-Domain Rotations
To achieve Journeyman licensure, an apprentice must complete and document verified operational hours across distinct operational domains during their 4-year (8,000-hour) progression:

* **Domain 1: Identity & Access Management (IAM):** 1,500 Hours (Directory services, PAM, authentication protocols, zero trust access controls).
* **Domain 2: Defensive Operations & SOC:** 2,500 Hours (Alert triage, SIEM correlation, incident handling, packet analysis, threat hunting).
* **Domain 3: Infrastructure & Systems Hardening:** 2,000 Hours (Vulnerability scanning, patch orchestration, firewall/cloud security group provisioning, endpoint baseline compliance).
* **Domain 4: Security Architecture & GRC Exposure:** 1,000 Hours (Threat modeling support, compliance evidence collection, risk register maintenance, security policy auditing).
* **Domain 5: Elective / Specialization Track:** 1,000 Hours (AppSec, Penetration Testing, IoMT / Medical Device Security, OT/ICS).

---

## 2. Legally Enforced Supervisory Ratios
To guarantee line-of-sight mentorship and prevent enterprises from replacing senior engineering staff with underpaid, unsupervised junior labor:

* **Standard Shift Ratio:** Maximum **2 Apprentices per 1 Licensed Journeyman** on any active operational shift or project team.
* **Master Tier Ratio:** A Master Practitioner may oversee up to **4 Journeymen** across complex architectural domains.
* **Contractor Compliance:** Managed Security Service Providers (MSSPs) and third-party contractors must maintain identical on-shift ratios within client tenant environments.
